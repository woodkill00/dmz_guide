# Maps, progression, community and security plan

Research date: 21 October 2026. Recommendations, not implementation. The lead verified the official 9 October 2026 MW4 DMZ guide, including Hajin, the Forward Operating Base and 3D Printer: https://www.callofduty.com/blog/2026/10/call-of-duty-modern-warfare-4-dmz-how-to-play-forward-operating-base-guide . Exact POIs, coordinates, recipe quantities and detailed mechanics remain unverified unless covered by the consolidated source matrix. This workstream relies on the lead’s verified primary-source reading for those game facts. No bot, account, credential or external communication was created. The requested MapGenie Urzikstan Zombies URL could not be retrieved by this workstream’s research tool. The lead separately reported that cloud-browser navigation loaded a title but observation was explicitly denied by the site-safety policy, without an approval prompt. Direct inspection stopped; no alternative route should bypass that denial. This report does not claim a visual/feature audit of the page; user-supplied screenshots could support a later review. Use it as a user-supplied interaction inspiration, not a source of licensed assets or validated MW4 facts.

## Executive decision

Ship a public, account-free guide with a 2D map and equivalent searchable POI list. Add private device-local goals, bookmarks and progress. Keep cloud accounts, published profiles, shared routes and Discord identity as separate opt-in capabilities. Use Leaflet CRS.Simple as the leading candidate, subject to a representative map/performance/accessibility spike, with original/rights-cleared map art. A static content build plus IndexedDB is sufficient for the first release; there is no requirement for a microservice, realtime collaboration, GIS server or Discord bot.

## Concrete proposals and tradeoffs

1. **Public map/list without sign-in.** Guides, filters, source dates and POI deep links are public. Pro: fast access on a second screen; no identity friction. Con: no automatic multi-device continuity. Priority: MVP.
2. **Leaflet CRS.Simple renderer.** Treat the game world as a square coordinate plane, not real-world latitude/longitude; use a single image initially and generated raster tiles when size demands. Pro: direct fit to game images and modest implementation burden. Con: large numbers of individual DOM markers need culling/clustering or canvas rendering. Priority: MVP. Leaflet explicitly documents non-geographic game maps, differing map/image units, and its [y,x] coordinate order: https://leafletjs.com/examples/crs-simple/crs-simple.html .
3. **Searchable POI list as a first-class view.** Each result includes category, floor, grid cell, landmark directions, confidence and status; selecting one updates the map. Pro: keyboard/screen-reader use, low bandwidth, useful when imagery is absent. Con: requires editorial landmark descriptions rather than map-only data. Priority: MVP.
4. **Mission/goal-aware filters.** “Useful for my selected goals” combines public locations with local needs, hiding completed nonrepeatable objectives only on request. Pro: reduces clutter without revealing inventory. Con: can mislead unless unknown/possible spawns remain labeled. Priority: MVP or first increment.
5. **Separate visit, completion and inventory actions.** Visited a site does not imply obtaining loot or finishing a task. Explicit quantity pickup, stash/carried destination and Undo; repeatable tasks have per-run resets. Pro: accurate personal state. Con: one extra action. Priority: MVP progression.
6. **Private local profiles, export/import and explicit reset.** IndexedDB for progress; versioned downloadable JSON backups; switchable profiles and previewed imports. Pro: inexpensive and private by default. Con: browser clearing/eviction can erase data; local does not mean encrypted or backed up. Priority: MVP. Browser persistence is not guaranteed: https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria .
7. **Curated routes before routing algorithms.** Editors author ordered POI sequences with prerequisites, floor transitions and alternatives. Pro: useful and understandable without pretending to know walkable geometry. Con: maintenance after map changes. Straight lines are itinerary connectors, not navigation. Priority: first increment.
8. **Scoped offline packs.** Explicitly download a selected map/version plus data and readable guides; show size, progress, last-update age and removal controls. Pro: resilient second-screen use. Con: storage and service-worker complexity; imagery rights must permit offline copies. Never prefetch all maps. Priority: after core reliability.
9. **Versioned evidence-backed content.** Stable POI/item IDs, patch applicability, reviewed sources, confidence, disputed/stale badges and changelogs. Pro: trust and reversible updates. Con: editorial workload. Priority: foundation, not later polish.
10. **Submission queue, not open publishing.** Community submits corrections and evidence; a reviewer decides before publication. Pro: crowdsourcing without instant misinformation/spam. Con: moderator capacity limits throughput. Begin with repository issue templates if community size permits. Priority: first increment.
11. **Discord invite and manually curated digest first.** A verified invite link plus guide deep links gives value without an app. Pro: no tokens or API permissions. Con: no in-client search or synchronized profile. Priority: launch if the owner has a server.
12. **Optional Discord slash-command search later.** /guide or /location returns public canonical links; scope to chosen servers/contexts. Pro: useful native discovery without reading conversations. Con: hosting, signature validation, rate-limit and policy maintenance. Commands can operate without installing a guild bot: https://docs.discord.com/developers/interactions/application-commands .
13. **Optional cloud sync, never an access gate.** Accounts synchronize only selected private data after clear consent; public sharing is a separate switch. Pro: cross-device convenience. Con: authorization, incident response, deletion and ongoing hosting obligations. Priority: only after demonstrated need.
14. **Source-linked patch impact queue.** Mark affected guides/POIs for re-review when a new content pack ships; keep old snapshots viewable and exclude stale rows from “verified current” filters. Pro: prevents silent outdated advice. Con: requires maintained dependency links. Priority: foundation with simple manual triggering.
15. **Public map view URLs without private state.** URLs encode map/version/floor/filters/selected POI. Private notes, goals, inventory and identity never enter a URL or analytics payload by default. Pro: shareable support/debugging and privacy. Con: users need a separate deliberate share flow for personal plans. Priority: MVP.
16. **Defer realtime squads, rankings and live telemetry.** Offer explicit text/JSON route export first. Pro: avoids cheating claims, unreliable unofficial integrations and moderation burden. Con: less social novelty. Priority: intentional non-goal until permission and verified APIs exist.

## Renderer decision and map data contract

Prefer Leaflet provisionally; finalize only after a representative spike validates coordinate transforms, mobile performance and accessible map/list behavior. Pin a tested stable release after checking the actual project dependencies; do not assume syntax from development-branch examples. Its rendering reference supports ImageOverlay, GridLayer/TileLayer and CRS.Simple: https://leafletjs.com/reference . MapLibre GL JS is the alternative if measured data volumes and styled vector layers warrant a WebGL pipeline, not an automatic upgrade: https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/ . It adds style/source/projection and GPU fallback complexity for a non-geographic game plane. Prototype with representative content before choosing. A PMTiles archive is a distribution choice, not by itself offline support.

Recommended JSON domain contract (custom planar schema, not mislabeled GeoJSON):

- MapManifest: schemaVersion, gameId, modeId, mapId, contentVersion, applicablePatch, coordinateSpaceId, bounds, origin=top-left, xDirection=right, yDirection=down, units=map-units, floors[], imagery[] and attribution/license records.
- Choose canonical extent 0..10000 in each axis for a square map, or an explicitly declared W/H ratio for a nonsquare map. This is a proposed abstract coordinate system, not in-game meters. Do not change it just because a higher-resolution image is uploaded.
- Each image/tile set declares crop/transform from image pixels to canonical space, dimensions, checksum, version, floor and rights record. Calibrate at least three non-collinear reference points; keep registration residual tolerance documented.
- POI: id (permanent), mapId, floorId, geometry point/line/polygon with x/y numeric pairs, categoryId, localizedName, aliases, description, landmarkDirections, gridCell, tags, relatedItemIds, relatedObjectiveIds, spawnKind=fixed/possible/event/unknown, prerequisites, availability, validFrom/validTo patch, verificationStatus, observedPatch, lastVerifiedAt, evidence[], editor/reviewer IDs, revision, supersedesId/deprecated flag.
- Evidence: source URL or authorized original media ID, sourceType=official/editor-observation/community-report, observationDate, applicablePatch, claim, attribution/permission status; private reporter details are not published.
- Layer: id, category IDs, floor, defaultVisibility, minZoom, symbol, accessible label, display priority. Layer instances remain provisional until their actual POIs and geometry are verified. Official confirmation of DMZ/crafting does not establish a specific location or drop table.
- Route: stable id, map/content version, ordered POI IDs and optional manually drawn connectors, floor transitions, prerequisites, difficulty/risk notes, source and last verified date. Do not label shortest/fastest without a validated traversable graph and metric.
- LocalProgress: profileId, schemaVersion, contentVersion, objective status, visited POIs, bookmarks, selected goals, inventory references, timestamp and per-run/reset scope. Use separate statuses and never derive objective completion solely from a map click.
- Leaflet adapter exclusively transforms canonical [x,y] into [mapHeight-y,x] when using an upward-positive plane. Test bounds/corners and floors. No component should handcraft coordinate swaps.
- Deep links resolve old stable IDs to a tombstone/replacement explanation, not a different POI. Migration previews show unknown IDs and preserve unmatched progress for recovery. A large geometry change creates a new revision/coordinate-space version instead of silently moving historic routes.

## Performance, offline, accessibility and search acceptance gates

These are proposed project budgets, not claims about existing performance. Validate on one named midrange Android device, iOS Safari and desktop Chrome/Firefox with representative data; report device/network/fixture sizes.

- Lazy-load map code only on map navigation; keep core guide/list HTML readable before map JS loads. Initial map should fetch low-resolution coverage first, then visible tiles. Do not download full-resolution world imagery by default.
- Use viewport culling, zoom-level clustering and a spatial index for large datasets; avoid thousands of popup/marker DOM nodes. Load only active map/floor layers; build search index per map. Move expensive filtering/indexing off the UI thread only when profiled.
- Proposed gates: normal mobile page LCP <=2.5 s at p75, INP <=200 ms; local search/filter response <=100 ms with a 5,000-POI fixture; avoid >100 ms main-thread tasks on map interactions. Do not promise all-device 60 FPS.
- Test 0/100/1,000/5,000 POIs, rapid floor/filter switching, slow or failed tile requests, WebGL absence if relevant, bad coordinates, portrait orientation and zoomed text. A performance regression blocks new heavy layers.
- Accessibility target WCAG 2.2 AA. Keyboard reaches search, filters, list, zoom controls and POI details without a trap; Escape closes dialogs and restores focus. No color-only categories/status; visible focus; icon text alternatives; screen-reader result announcements; reduced-motion behavior; touch controls target 44 CSS px as a design goal. The list must contain equivalent actionable information, not simply say “see map.” W3C criteria: https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/quickref/ .
- Search normalizes aliases and spelling variants, supports category/floor/version filters, ranks exact names first, and announces why a result is hidden by filters. No-result state offers clearing filters. Ensure search and map selection stay in sync and the Back button restores view.
- Offline scope: app shell plus selected rights-cleared map pack and search data; private progress remains in IndexedDB. Atomic pack downloads with version/checksum validation; incomplete download never replaces a working pack. Separate static/offline caches from authenticated responses. Deletion/log-out clears applicable account caches. Test interrupted update, quota exceeded, eviction, service-worker upgrade and reopening offline. Show “saved on this device; export a backup” rather than “safe forever.”

## Rights and editorial safeguards

MapGenie inspiration does not grant rights to its tiles, icons, code, descriptions, compiled POI dataset or screenshots. Do not scrape, hotlink, redistribute or build an offline pack from those assets without documented permission. Independently author content and map styling; keep a rights ledger for every image, icon and imported dataset. A self-drawn reconstruction can still raise derivative-work issues, so “we drew it” is not blanket clearance. Do not assume a repository code license covers game imagery.

Activision reserves rights and its terms restrict copying/reuse and unauthorized data extraction; this is a release risk requiring rights review, not a conclusion that any particular fan-guide use is unlawful. The safe production gate is documented permission/license or reviewed permissible use for the intended distribution, commercial status and offline use. Until cleared, use an original abstract placeholder diagram and text/list view. Credit/“unofficial fan guide” notices are appropriate but do not replace permission. Relevant primary terms: https://www.activision.com/legal/terms-of-use .

Editorial flow: contributor draft -> schema/evidence/duplicate checks -> reviewer verifies claim and patch -> preview -> publish immutable content build -> release changelog -> scheduled/manual re-verification. Roles: contributor cannot publish; editor can revise; publisher approves production; moderator manages reports; administrator manages access. Small teams may combine editor/publisher, but require second review for large imports/permission changes. Preserve revision history and reasons, support rollback, and distinguish “official”, “editor tested”, “community reported” and “unverified.” Popularity is not evidence. Screenshots must remove personal identifiers/chat unless permission exists. No covert server history harvesting; Discord submissions are opt-in, not assumed reusable public content.

## Discord ladder and least privilege

Level 0: verified invite + public guide links, no API. Level 1: optionally a one-way release-note webhook to one owner-approved channel; URL is a secret, stored server-side, never in client bundles/repository. Approval/configuration is separate from this planning task. Level 2: public slash-command search using applications.commands; prefer HTTP interactions and no general-message ingestion. Level 3: optional account link for private cloud sync using identify only when identity is needed; no email/connections/guilds scopes by default. Level 4: restricted moderation/community workflow only if demonstrated need and separately authorized, never Administrator permissions. Public guides stay usable at every level without Discord.

For OAuth use a maintained server-side library, authorization-code flow, state bound to session, exact redirect allowlist, secure HttpOnly SameSite session cookies and server-side token storage; verify provider-supported security options at implementation time rather than assuming PKCE support. Discord scopes and flows: https://docs.discord.com/developers/topics/oauth2 .

Interaction endpoints must verify Ed25519 signature and timestamp against the raw request body, reject invalid requests, and handle duplicate interaction IDs idempotently; use a bounded replay/timestamp policy and defer longer responses per current API deadlines. Primary security documentation: https://github.com/discord/discord-api-docs/blob/main/developers/interactions/overview.mdx . Honor Retry-After and dynamic rate-limit headers instead of hardcoding rates: https://github.com/discord/discord-api-docs/blob/main/developers/topics/rate-limits.mdx .

Do not use self-bots, user tokens, message-content/member/presence intents for guide search, automatic joins, or broad server scraping. User-generated command parameters are untrusted and must not render HTML or inject mentions; suppress allowed mentions by default. No public display of private progress through commands unless the user deliberately chooses to share that exact view. Bot/webhook availability must not block guides. Policy review gate: https://support-dev.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/8563934450327-Discord-Developer-Policy .

## Threat model and release tests

Assets: integrity of published guides, local progress, future private account records, editor credentials, Discord tokens/webhook URLs, reputation and hosting budget. Trust boundaries: public content -> renderer; import file -> local state; user session -> API/database; contributor -> publisher; Discord callback -> command handler; deployment pipeline -> production.

- Stored/reflected XSS through POI names, markdown, links or imports: render text by default, allowlisted Markdown sanitizer, reject dangerous protocols/raw HTML, strict CSP as defense in depth. Test script/event-handler/javascript-URL payloads in every field. https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cross_Site_Scripting_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html .
- Insecure direct object reference in future sync: enforce owner/role checks server-side on every read/write/export/delete; client-supplied ownerId is not authority. Test two unrelated accounts plus anonymous access, role downgrade and revoked sessions. https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html .
- Malicious imports: bounded file size, entity count, nesting/string length, finite coordinates, referential integrity, schema version allowlist, duplicate-ID handling and preview before merge. Never evaluate code, fetch remote URLs, accept arbitrary SVG, or use imported object keys unsafely. Test prototype-pollution keys, huge quantities, NaN-like values, path traversal names and unresolved refs.
- SSRF/media abuse: no server-side arbitrary evidence-URL previews or remote image downloads initially. Later uploads require type verification, byte/pixel limits, metadata stripping and isolated storage; no executable formats.
- Account/session abuse: CSRF defenses for state-changing APIs, OAuth state mismatch/replay tests, exact redirect tests, rate limits and log redaction. No secrets in localStorage or front-end environment variables.
- Content poisoning/spam/harassment: moderation queue, reporting and appeal route, contributor quotas and audit trail; no open private messaging or public user profiles initially. Reviewer capacity is a launch gate for submissions.
- Supply-chain/deployment risk: lockfile, dependency scanning, protected production branch, minimal CI permissions, secret scan, separate preview/prod secrets and tested rollback. Build content as data, never executable community templates.
- Privacy leak: share preview explicitly enumerates included fields; exclude inventory/notes from URLs, logs, analytics and Discord output. Opt-in account data minimization, export/delete and documented retention; do not collect DOB, real name or contact list for progress tracking.
- Loss/conflicting writes: local export/import round-trip tests, schema migration fixtures, independent-tab changes and interrupted writes. Cloud sync later uses version checks and visible conflicts, not silent last-write-wins on inventory counts.

## Phased delivery and decision gates

Phase 0: reconcile verified game facts from the official source matrix, verify remaining POI/recipe details, repository state, asset rights and intended audience. Agree content schema and create a synthetic, clearly labeled demo map/pack. Exit: named rights owner for every shipped asset and no invented target-game claims.
Phase 1: public guides + one map/list + categories/search/deep links + private progress/export/import. Exit: coordinate calibration, search parity, accessibility and malicious-input tests pass; reset/migration cannot silently destroy progress.
Phase 2: curated routes, goals-to-POI filters, patch/source workflow, moderated corrections, optional verified Discord invite. Exit: stale-data indicators and review/rollback process work; moderator capacity exists.
Phase 3: measured offline need and performance optimizations. Exit: rights permit caching, offline/version/storage-failure tests pass and payload budgets met.
Phase 4, only with demand: opt-in accounts/cloud sync and narrow Discord commands. Exit: privacy notice, deletion/export, cross-user authorization tests, secret handling, abuse limits, monitoring and recovery procedure independently reviewed. Defer realtime squad sync, public progression leaderboards, automatic game-account telemetry and complex routing until they have proven user value and verified authorized inputs.

## Second-round review: additional requirements under reconciliation

### Coordinates and content evolution

Distinguish three independent versions: content version (claims/POIs), imagery version (resolution/art/crop), coordinate-space version (geometry registration). Replacing a higher-resolution image should preserve the plane and update its image transform only. Correcting the plane itself requires an explicit migration and route/progress preview. Keep original geometry and old route snapshot until the user accepts conversion; validate control-point residuals and floor identity. Deleting a POI is a tombstone, not reassignment of its ID.

### Goal filters, spoilers and sharing

Goal-aware filters run locally by default; absence of a verified drop relationship means “location data unknown,” not “no locations.” Map results identify possible versus fixed sources without inferring undisclosed drop probabilities. Spoiler preferences apply to autocomplete, search snippets, result counts where revealing, labels, URLs, route names, thumbnails and Discord responses. Add a per-result reveal affordance. Story/campaign guides and checklists are first-class but use separate game/mode/campaign/chapter namespaces and reset policies, not a universal DMZ goal ledger. Recipe planning remains the first deeply implemented progression workflow.

Share previews enumerate included fields. A public view URL may contain map/patch/layer/POI references, but not private inventory, notes, active goals or derived “missing item” counts unless explicitly selected. Even a POI list can imply a goal: warn when sharing a goal-derived itinerary, and offer a generic location view. Client-side fragments are not a privacy guarantee because recipients and page scripts can read them.

### Untrusted community reports and cached packs

Contributor reputation can prioritize review, not convert assertions into fact. Require independent reproducible evidence for contested claims; two reviewers reading the same recycled screenshot are not independent corroboration. Detect duplicate evidence, record contributor conflicts and preserve disputes. Editors can temporarily hide harmful/incorrect content pending investigation. No automatic publication based on votes or trusted-user score.

Use a small first-party current-pack/revocation manifest on reconnect with pack IDs and reasons. Quarantine packs flagged unsafe, distinguish security invalidation from ordinary outdated data, stop execution/rendering of unsafe content, and retain private progress for safe migration/export. Never delete the user's records merely because a content pack is revoked. A revision hash detects accidental change, not malicious authorship; a trusted release channel and review are still required. Avoid introducing a custom PKI until justified.

Offline the app cannot discover a new revocation. Show last online validation timestamp and “updates/security status not checked while offline,” not “current/safe.” A previously known revoked pack stays blocked offline. Merely old editorial data may remain readable with stale warnings; a known security-invalid pack must not. Test reconnect after revocation, old service-worker caches, interrupted manifest refresh, unavailable validation endpoint and a user reopening a quarantined pack from backup.

### Identity linking and sync

Discord OAuth identity is neither proof of game ownership nor authorization to import/merge/publish progress. Linking an identity must not import inventory automatically. Snapshot imports need explicit replace/merge-by-field/conflict resolution and a preview; never sum two snapshots that may represent the same inventory. Per-operation UUIDs provide deduplication only for actual operation logs; they cannot make duplicate snapshots safe to add. Future account sync should keep private datasets owner-scoped, show merge provenance and permit disconnect/export without publishing them.

### Actual crafting-review response and resolved tradeoffs

The crafting reviewer accepted the five challenges with concrete refinements: snapshots default to replacing a selected profile after a reviewed diff, never arithmetic merging; event IDs deduplicate only actual event logs. Unknown/not-reviewed locations remain distinct from verified absence. Share preview strips goal names/counts/private notes and applies spoiler settings. Image replacements and coordinate migrations remain separate; unresolved routes are quarantined rather than reinterpreted. Game/mode/profile/season namespaces prevent campaign resets affecting DMZ.

The deliberate tradeoff is fewer effortless shares and automatic merges in exchange for truthful inventory and stronger privacy. The crafting reviewer also identified verified secure-backpack retention from the official FOB guide: a failed-exfil action removes only unprotected carried items, not all carried items. Map actions must invoke this same inventory engine contract rather than maintain a second conflicting loss rule.

### Actual delivery-review disagreement and resolution

The delivery reviewer proposed JSON manifests plus trusted hosted or separately selected local bounded rasters for MVP and deferring ZIP import. This workstream challenged calling manifest-only export portable picture packs: it preserves references, not the pictures. Resolved wording is **reference pack**, with clear missing-asset diagnostics and explicit local relinking. A minimum picture reference includes stable asset ID/checksum, dimensions/type, rights/provenance, logical reference and image-to-canonical transform. No arbitrary remote fetch occurs during import. Bounded raster bundle completion is scheduled in the delivery plan’s S4 before claiming the self-contained picture-pack feature complete; ZIP decoding remains deferred until resource limits and isolation are proven. This is a real scope/terminology correction, not a claim that portability is already delivered.

The delivery reviewer accepted coordinate-space migration protection, independent evidence instead of trust-score publication, cross-surface spoiler filtering and separate campaign/story/DMZ namespaces. They also accepted known-unsafe pack blocking, ordinary-stale warnings, reconnect validation and offline last-validation disclosure without deleting progress. Explicit costs: additional asset storage/security work, no guarantee of offline trust freshness, increased editorial workload and intentionally reduced search completeness when spoilers are hidden.

Renderer reconciliation: Leaflet CRS.Simple remains the leading candidate, not an unconditional architectural lock. A representative spike must pass transforms, mobile interaction, large-POI and accessible-list gates before an ADR finalizes it; MapLibre is the fallback candidate if measurements justify its extra pipeline complexity.
